Are You Getting Enough Sleep?
1. Randy Gardner was a high school student in the United States. He wanted to find out what happens when you don’t get enough sleep. He did an experiment for his school project. With the help of Dr. William C. Dement from Stanford University and two friends, Gardner stayed awake for 264 hours and 12 minutes. That is 11 days and nights without sleep.
2. After 24 hours without sleep, Gardner started to have trouble reading and watching TV. The pictures and words looked blurry. By the third day, he had trouble using his hands. On the fourth day, he started to see things that were not real. For example, he thought a street sign was a person. He also imagined he was a football player. His speech became unclear, and by the eleventh day, he could not finish a simple counting test. He forgot what he was doing in the middle of the test.
3. When Gardner finally went to bed, he slept for 14 hours and 45 minutes. The next night, he slept for 12 hours, and the night after that, he slept for 10 and a half hours. By the fourth night, his sleep schedule was normal again. Even though he recovered quickly, scientists say not getting enough sleep can be dangerous.
4. Scientists say we should not repeat Randy’s experiment. They say lack of sleep can have serious effects. For example, tests on rats showed that after a few weeks without sleep, the rats lost their fur and weight. Even though they ate more food, they still lost weight. Eventually, the rats died because they had no sleep.
5. Randy’s experiment is not the longest anyone has stayed awake. Maureen Weston from the United Kingdom holds the record for staying awake the longest. She stayed awake for 449 hours in 1977, which is 18 days and 17 hours. During your life, you will likely sleep for more than 25 years. Scientists still don’t know exactly why we need sleep, but they know it is important for our brains and bodies to stay healthy.
