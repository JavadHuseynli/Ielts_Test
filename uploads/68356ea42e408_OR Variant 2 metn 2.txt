					
					READING PASSAGE 2

				Generation Z: Digital Natives

1. Yesim Yilmaz, a high school student in Istanbul, starts her day by checking her email on her phone while having breakfast, which her mother brings to her. Realizing that she forgot to read a chapter for her biology class, Yesim opens her laptop and quickly downloads the chapter from her online textbook. She reads it while on the train to school.
2. In Seoul, Min-ho Park waits for the bus on a Sunday afternoon. As he types a text to his friend to let them know he’s on his way, Min-ho laughs at funny photos his friend posted online. He has already bought tickets for a movie on his phone, which he and his friends will see later. On the bus, he opens a game app, puts in his earphones, and relaxes, just like most other people his age around him.
3. Yesim and Min-ho are part of Generation Z, also called "digital natives" because they have grown up with the internet, mobile phones, and social media. Born between the mid-1990s and early 2000s, they have always had access to technology. They are connected online daily for work, entertainment, and staying in touch with friends. This generation is also sometimes referred to as Generation C, with “C” standing for content, community, or creativity.
4. While their parents used cassette players and landline phones as teenagers, Generation Z is constantly online. Research shows that young people in Asia spend an average of 9.5 hours a day online. Companies know this and target Gen Z with ads tailored to their interests. Social media sites work with marketing companies to gather information about users, such as their favorite products and hobbies, so they can show them personalized advertisements.
5. Even though many Gen Z members know that companies collect a lot of personal information, they aren’t very concerned about it. They’re more focused on keeping their private lives hidden from their parents. For example, Valerie Chen in Kaohsiung is upset because her parents want to know everything she does online. However, she knows how to limit what they can see on her social media accounts. There is also the issue of social media posts affecting future job opportunities. Studies show that more than 70% of companies have rejected candidates based on what they’ve seen online. Generation Z may be better at managing their online privacy, but only time will tell how they adapt to the challenges of a digitally connected world.
